P12 D6 (backend): the wallet over HTTP, and three cards that were wrong

The Mini App could show a market and place an order; it could not show a wallet. D6's
custody UX existed only in the chat, and five ledger rows said `built: false`. This
commit is the HTTP half of ceremonies the bot has run since P07 — six routes, one test
file that walks the ceremony in order, and the three chat money cards that turned out to
be broken in ways reading them could not show.

Six routes (services/api/app.py, contract, `check-openapi` table):

  GET  /v1/wallet/balance            the card: custody, cash, reserved, chains, locks
  GET  /v1/wallet/transactions       the statement, cursor-paginated, reason verbatim
  POST /v1/wallet/deposit/quote      202 + a `deposits` row in `detecting`, keyed
  GET  /v1/wallet/deposit/{id}       the four legs as states, plus `problem` when stuck
  POST /v1/wallet/withdraw           202, the ceremony in the order the locks are
  POST /v1/wallet/keys/export        the wrapped DEK once, counted against its budget

Four codes join `CODES`, the Python refusal table, the Mini App's copy of it, the
contract's `Error.code` enum and the key-parity gate: `INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE`,
`PASSWORD_REQUIRED`, `PASSWORD_WRONG`, `ADDRESS_NOT_ALLOWED`.

Three bugs found by writing the tests, all of them live before this commit:

1. `cash_ledger` has `amount_micro`/`created_ms`. The chat cards and the first draft of
   these routes read `delta_micro`/`at_ms` — names that read better and do not exist, so
   `/balance`, `/history` and `/pnl` raised on every tap. `_tm.usdc(...)` was called in
   the same cards and does not exist anywhere; the formatter is `fmt_usdc`.
2. `/pnl` summed `kind='realised'` (never a ledger kind in the CHECK) and read
   `position_snapshots.balance_micro` (never a column). It would have answered 0.00
   forever. Realised is now exits + settlements minus the basis they released minus our
   fees, from the ledger and the lots; the peak is cash, and the card says cash.
3. `/history` printed the kind and the amount and dropped the reason — the one field the
   ledger entry exists for.

And one ordering bug in the withdrawal route itself, caught by the retry test:
`_totp_gate` consumes an authenticator step, and the ceremony used to run before
`_idem_run`, so the retry of a withdrawal whose response was lost came back
`TOTP_INVALID` — a completed withdrawal reported as a failed one. The locks now run
inside the key: a replay is answered from the stored body, and the test asserts the
enrolment's `last_step` did not move.

Also here: `_OPEN_INTENT_STATES` (the reserve used to list `live`/`partial`, which are
*orders* states, so `pending`/`submitting`/`uncertain` — money genuinely committed — were
missing), `INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE` joins `_PUBLIC_DETAIL_CODES` so the refusal can say what
is available, a stopped deposit no longer paints five green ticks, and the export gets a
real response object for its `no-store` header.

`tests/test_wallet_api.py`: 51 tests. The withdrawal tests are a ladder — each rung
breaks one lock and asserts the rung above it was reached — because the order of the
locks is the feature. `TestWalletCards` calls the cards directly with money in the
tables, which is the only way a tap-only surface gets tested at all.

Verified: pytest 1284 passed; check-openapi 659/0; p12-gate-check 31/0; sqlite
migrations --check clean.
