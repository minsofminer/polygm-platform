P12 D6 (web, part 1): the wallet's QR is built here, and this time the reference was the one that was wrong

A deposit address is the one thing on the wallet screen a person scans rather than types, so the QR is the
safer input path and it is not decoration: a matrix that is almost right scans into an address nobody owns.
`web/src/tma/qr.ts` is a dependency-free byte-mode/level-M/versions-1-10 encoder (14 to 213 bytes: an address,
an EIP-681 URI, a short bot link), with a typed `QrTooLongError` rather than a truncation, and `qrSvgPath`
for the webview to render with no network.

The interesting part is how the fixtures were finally pinned. `qr.test.ts` compares every cell of three
matrices (`address` v3, `eip681` v4, `long` v8 — one block, two blocks, four unequal blocks plus the version
header) against a second implementation. Two cases failed, and the failure was in the error-correction tail,
not in the data. Bisecting it out took four separate suspects, and three of them were mine:

  * the candidate mask was being scored with the format information already drawn; ISO/IEC 18004 section 7.8
    says to evaluate the data region, so the header is now written after the choice, not before;
  * the Annex B rule-3 penalty searched for the eleven-module sequence `10111010000`, which only matches
    patterns whose four light modules are *inside* the symbol — every match flush against an edge, which is
    most of the score on a small symbol, was missed. Rewritten as the seven-module core plus a light area on
    one side or the symbol edge;
  * my own "is this stream Reed-Solomon clean" check had the wrong roots (they are alpha^0..alpha^(ec-1));
  * and the actual cause of the two failures: **segno**, the reference, appends `8 - (length % 8)` padding
    bits, which is a whole extra zero codeword when the bit stream already ends on a codeword boundary.
    ISO/IEC 18004 7.4.10 pads only as far as the boundary, and only if it is not there already. Invisible to
    a decoder, but it shifted 24 codewords and the whole EC tail on two of the three cases, and it moved the
    eip681 symbol's chosen mask from 2 to 6. Verified for every text length from 1 to 13 bytes; the two
    encoders agree at 14, where the stream fills the block and carries no pad codewords at all — which is
    exactly why `address` passed from the start.

`tools/build-qr-fixtures.py` now patches that deviation out, cross-checks every fixture's codeword stream
against `qrcode`'s byte for byte, and reads each generated matrix back — format information, codeword walk,
de-interleave, syndromes, payload — before writing it, because a fixture is only worth comparing against if
it is a real QR. All three fixtures are now the same symbol both encoders describe. The tool also records
*why* segno is the mask authority and `qrcode` is not: they score the candidates in different states, and
the spec's own rule is the one segno implements.

Also in this pass:
  * `qr.ts` rewritten clean under `noUncheckedIndexedAccess` (57 errors): table and matrix reads go through
    `lg`/`al` and `darkAt`/`fixedAt`, and the two writes throw rather than silently dropping a module.
  * `tools/p12-gate-check.py` gains a fourth section: the encoder still imports nothing, still fetches
    nothing, the test still compares every cell of every case in the file, each fixture is a square matrix of
    the size it claims, the fixtures on disk are still the ones the generator produces, and two canaries for
    the fixture shapes that would make those checks vacuous. 31 -> 41 checks.

Verified: `npx vitest run` 58 files / 511 tests green (qr.test.ts 9/9), `npx tsc --noEmit` clean,
`python3 tools/p12-gate-check.py` 41/0, `--self-test` 4/4 caught.
